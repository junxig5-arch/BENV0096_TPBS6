# Draft Text for P4-P5 Analysis

## Data and accounting basis

The P4-P5 analysis uses the DESNZ Energy and Emissions Projections (EEP) 2024-2050 as the current-policy baseline and compares this with target-consistent pathways from the Climate Change Committee (CCC). DESNZ annual emissions were extracted from Annex A, Territorial Emissions Statistics (TES) sectors, Reference scenario. Two DESNZ totals are retained: total greenhouse gas emissions excluding international aviation and shipping (IAS), and total greenhouse gas emissions including IAS. This distinction is necessary because a consistent historical territorial series is best represented excluding IAS, while the CCC economy-wide pathway totals are closer to an including-IAS comparison.

Official carbon-budget performance is treated separately from annual pathway comparison. DESNZ Web Table 2.1 is used for official carbon-budget-period metrics. In line with UK carbon budget accounting, CB4 and CB5 are compared on an excluding-IAS basis, while CB6 is compared on a Net Carbon Account / including-IAS basis. This avoids treating differences in accounting scope as substantive emissions differences.

For the CCC benchmark, the main comparison uses the CCC Seventh Carbon Budget full dataset, Economy-wide data sheet, Balanced Pathway scenario, United Kingdom, variable "Emissions: direct emissions total". The CCC Sixth Carbon Budget Balanced Net Zero Pathway is retained as a secondary benchmark to show that the broad 2050 gap estimate is not sensitive to the choice of CCC dataset.

## P4: DESNZ current-policy baseline and carbon-budget gap

DESNZ EEP 2024 projects UK territorial greenhouse gas emissions excluding IAS to fall from 363.2 MtCO2e in 2025 to 290.2 MtCO2e in 2050. When IAS is included, the 2050 baseline is 324.3 MtCO2e. This means that the current-policy baseline does not approach net zero by 2050, even before considering uncertainty around policy delivery.

On the official carbon-budget accounting basis, DESNZ projects the UK to remain within CB4 and CB5 but to miss CB6 substantially. The projected official comparison emissions are 1824.1 MtCO2e for CB4 against a budget of 1,950 MtCO2e, and 1638.6 MtCO2e for CB5 against a budget of 1,725 MtCO2e. In contrast, projected CB6 emissions are 1702.5 MtCO2e against a budget of 965 MtCO2e, implying an official projected shortfall of 737.5 MtCO2e over 2033-2037. On a territorial excluding-IAS basis, the CB6 gap is smaller but still large, at 546.6 MtCO2e.

## P5: DESNZ versus CCC benchmark comparison

The DESNZ including-IAS pathway initially sits close to the CCC Seventh Carbon Budget Balanced Pathway, but the pathways diverge sharply after the late 2020s. In 2030, DESNZ including-IAS emissions are 368.0 MtCO2e, compared with 295.8 MtCO2e in CCC7, a gap of 72.2 MtCO2e. By 2035 the gap widens to 154.8 MtCO2e, and by 2050 it reaches 325.4 MtCO2e.

The CCC Sixth Carbon Budget Balanced Net Zero Pathway gives a very similar 2050 comparison. DESNZ including-IAS emissions exceed the CCC6 pathway by 325.3 MtCO2e in 2050. This supports the interpretation that the central result is robust: under current-policy projections, the UK remains roughly 325 MtCO2e above a net-zero-consistent 2050 pathway when IAS is included.

The carbon-budget-period comparison shows the same pattern in cumulative form. CCC7 sums to 944.2 MtCO2e over CB6, slightly below the statutory CB6 level of 965 MtCO2e. DESNZ official comparison emissions for CB6 are 1702.5 MtCO2e, leaving a large policy-delivery gap before 2050.

## Interpretation

The main analytical message from P4-P5 is that the UK emissions gap is already visible before 2050. DESNZ current-policy projections are not simply a little above a target-consistent endpoint; they diverge across the 2030s, especially around CB6. This makes the 2050 gap a consequence of insufficient medium-term delivery rather than only a distant endpoint problem. The accounting distinction is central: excluding IAS gives a consistent territorial trend, but policy conclusions about carbon-budget performance should use the official NCA basis.
