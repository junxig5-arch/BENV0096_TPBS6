1. Revised Research Questions 
The dissertation is centred on quantifying the UK 2050 emissions gap and explaining how sectoral drivers and uncertainty 
assumptions affect that gap.

3. Uncertainty Framework 
Uncertainty will be treated as expanding towards 2050, similar to a “trumpet” or “cone”. The aim is not to build a full energ y system 
model, but to interpret the DESNZ baseline within a structured range of plausible policy and technology futures.

4. Accounting Methodology Note 
The accounting basis must be explicit because UK carbon budget performance, historical emissions and projection series do not  
always use the same treatment of International Aviation and Shipping (IAS). 
Purpose Accounting basis Reason 
Official carbon budget performance DESNZ Net Carbon Account (NCA) basis Matches the official method used to assess 
carbon budget performance. 
Historical-to-projection trend figures Territorial emissions excluding IAS Provides a consistent time-series comparison and 
avoids mixing accounting boundaries. 
CB6 gap interpretation Report both official NCA-based and excluding-IAS 
values where relevant 
Makes the difference transparent and avoids 
treating different accounting questions as 
conflicting results. 
International / imported emissions Discussion or limitation only Relevant to wider UK climate responsibility, but 
not the main basis for UK carbon budgets or 
DESNZ EEP. 
Working decision: use the official DESNZ NCA basis for policy conclusions on carbon budget performance, while using excluding -IAS 
territorial emissions for consistent historical-projection trend visualisation. Where the distinction matters, especially for CB6, both 
values will be reported and explained.
